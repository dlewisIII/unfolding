This is a research-based translation of the first sūtra of the *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* and its accompanying commentary by Vyāsa. The work serves as the first practical test of the method described in the [methodology note](https://unfolding.day/entries/patanjalayogashastra-methodology).

The research proceeded in stages: examination of the Sanskrit text, morphological analysis, verification of meanings against dictionaries, direct translation from Sanskrit into Russian and English, and finally, a cross-check against James Haughton Woods’s 1914 translation.

**How to read.** Each passage presents the original Sanskrit text in Devanāgarī script, followed by its IAST transliteration and English translation. Where choosing a single English equivalent would prematurely narrow the meaning of a word, the Sanskrit term is retained in transliteration and italicized. The meanings of these terms are explained in the notes. A detailed grammatical analysis and source references are available separately for readers wishing to explore the text in greater depth. The reasons behind this approach to translation will be discussed in detail in a separate note.

**Notation.** *YS* stands for *Yoga Sūtra*; *Vyāsabhāṣya* (“Vyāsa’s Commentary”) is the traditional name of the commentary attributed to Vyāsa. In both headings, the designation 1.1 refers to the first sūtra of the first chapter and its commentary. The commentary is divided into separate passages solely for ease of reading.

## YS 1.1

> अथ योगानुशासनम् ॥
>
> *atha yogānuśāsanam*

*atha* — instruction on yoga.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*atha* is an inceptive particle: “here, now [begins]”; at the opening of a work it is also a sign of an auspicious beginning. The first passage of the commentary explains it. “Instruction” renders *anuśāsana*: “instruction, direction, laying down of rules, treatment of a subject”. The dictionary range of *yoga* is very wide: joining; application, practice; means, method; concentration, contemplation; the name of a doctrine. The third passage of the commentary narrows it through *samādhi*.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *atha | yoga-anuśāsanam*; at the compound boundary *a + a → ā*.
- *atha*: indeclinable, a particle opening the utterance. MW (L 3531): “auspicious and inceptive particle”; now, then, moreover, rather, certainly, but, else. Apte (L 889), sense 1: at the beginning of works, “here, now (begins)”; *maṅgala*, *ārambha*, *adhikāra*. Apte notes that auspiciousness is not a lexical sense but the effect of uttering the word. Other senses (then; if; and; question; totality; doubt) are not supported by the structure of the sūtra. Open: inceptive/heading, “now/here”, auspicious.
- *yogānuśāsanam*: compound with final head *anuśāsana*; neuter singular; nominative or accusative cannot be decided, since there is no predicate. The internal relation of *yoga* to *anuśāsana* is not formally expressed; Apte allows the object of *anuśāsana* to stand in a compound (“instruction on …”).
- *yoga* (MW L 172001, 172324–61; Apte L 27127): joining, connection; application, practice; means, method; effort; concentration of thought, contemplation (MW; Apte sense 28); Patañjali’s system (Apte sense 29); many concrete and technical senses unsupported by the context.
- *anuśāsana* (MW L 7397; Apte L 2155): instruction, direction, command, precept; advice; laying down rules or precepts; treatment (of a subject).
- No verb is supplied. “Yoga” is written as an ordinary English word in the natural phrase “instruction on yoga”; in terminological definition and conceptual analysis (from the third passage of the commentary onward) it stays *yoga* in IAST. This parallels the author’s decision for the Russian text. The heading-like sentence has no article (“instruction”, not “the instruction”), as the Sanskrit has none.

</details>

## VYĀSABHĀṢYA 1.1

> अथेत्य् अयम् अधिकारार्थः ।
>
> *athety ayam adhikārārthaḥ*

The word *atha* here has the meaning *adhikāra*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*adhikāra*: “topic, section; relation, reference; scope; governing rule”; also “authority, right, office”, which the context does not support. “Meaning” renders *artha* in the compound *adhikārārtha*.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *atha + iti | ayam | adhikāra + arthaḥ*. Sandhi: *atha + iti → atheti*; *iti + ayam → ity ayam*; *adhikāra + arthaḥ → adhikārārthaḥ*.
- *atha iti*: the quoted word; *iti* marks the quotation.
- *ayam*: *idam*, masc. nom. sg.; subject, pointing to the quoted *atha*.
- *adhikārārthaḥ*: masc. nom. sg., nominal predicate; zero copula. MW and Apte have no separate entry *adhikārārtha*; the similar *adhikārtha* is *adhika + artha*, a different word.
- *adhikāra* (MW L 4182–94; Apte L 1042): authority, government; office, rank; claim, right; reference, relation; topic, subject; paragraph, section; in grammar, a governing rule whose force extends over the following rules. The context fits relation, topic/section, scope, governing rule; no choice among them is made.
- *artha* (MW L 15840–54; Apte L 4564): aim, purpose; cause; use; thing, object; sense, meaning; affair; wealth, etc. “Sense, meaning” is strongest here (the subject is a quoted word); a purposive sense of the final member (“for, for the sake of”) is possible. A residual formal possibility: the adjective *ārtha*.
- Approved wording follows the Russian: “The word *atha* here has the meaning *adhikāra*” (*artha* = “meaning”). “The word” and “here” render *atha iti ayam* (“this [word] *atha*”).
- Alternatives: purposive *artha* (“*atha* is for the sake of *adhikāra*”); keeping *adhikārārtha* in IAST.

</details>

> योगानुशासनं शास्त्रम् अधिकृतं वेदितव्यम् ।
>
> *yogānuśāsanaṃ śāstram adhikṛtaṃ veditavyam*

It is to be understood that the *śāstra*, the instruction on yoga, has been taken as the subject of the exposition.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*śāstra*: “teaching, treatise, body of knowledge; rule, precept”. “Taken as the subject” renders *adhikṛta* (“made the subject”); it is a word of the same root as *adhikāra* in the preceding passage.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *yoga-anuśāsanam | śāstram | adhi-kṛtam | veditavyam*; all four are neuter nominative singular.
- Structure: *śāstram* is the subject; *yogānuśāsanam* is in apposition to it; *adhikṛtam* is a secondary predicate; *veditavyam* is the predicate.
- *adhikṛtam*: past passive participle of *adhi-√kṛ*. Apte (L 1036), sense 3: “make the subject of”, with a passive example with an inanimate subject: *nāṭakaṃ prayogeṇa adhikriyatām*. MW *adhi-kṛtya* (L 4208): “having made the chief subject”. Personal senses (“appoint, authorize”) are excluded by the inanimate *śāstram*.
- No sense “begin, commence” occurs in any entry of the *adhikṛ-/adhikār-* family in MW and Apte; Woods’s “commenced” is not adopted. For the same reason the English avoids “taken up”, which can suggest commencement.
- *veditavyam*: gerundive of *√vid*; MW (L 206062–63): “to be known or recognized as, taken for”. Hence “It is to be understood”.
- *śāstra* (MW L 216350–53; Apte L 32007): order, rule, precept; teaching; manual, treatise; body of teaching, science; scripture.
- “Of the exposition” is not expressed in the Sanskrit; it is an explanatory addition carried over from the approved Russian wording.

</details>

> योगः समाधिः ।
>
> *yogaḥ samādhiḥ*

*yoga* is *samādhi*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*samādhi*: “putting together, joining; concentration, being concentrated; profound contemplation, absorption”; also “completion, agreement”.

The definition is directional: *yoga* is characterized through *samādhi*. The converse, that every *samādhi* is *yoga*, does not follow.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- *yogaḥ*, *samādhiḥ*: masc. nom. sg.; a nominal sentence with zero copula. *yogaḥ* is the definiendum (subject), *samādhiḥ* the definiens (predicate). English requires the copula “is”; it adds no content.
- *samādhi* (MW L 234154–67; Apte L 33812): putting together, joining, union; completion, settlement, agreement; intense application or fixing of the mind, attention; concentration of thoughts, profound or abstract meditation, absorption; religious vow, devotion; other lexicalized senses.
- The sentence narrows *yoga* to the field of concentration, contemplation and absorption; the precise sub-sense remains open.

</details>

> स च सार्वभौमश् चित्तस्य धर्मः ।
>
> *sa ca sārvabhaumaś cittasya dharmaḥ*

And this is a *sārvabhauma* *dharma* of *citta*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*sārvabhauma* is derived from *sarva-bhūmi* (“the whole earth”): “universal, world-wide”; dictionaries also give “relating to all conditions of the mind”, but that sense comes from the Yoga tradition itself. *dharma* here: “nature, property, quality, condition”. *citta*: “attention, thought, intention, mind, heart, memory”; the text gives no substantive definition.

“This” (*sa*) may refer to *yoga* or to *samādhi*; the text does not specify.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *sa(ḥ) | ca | sārvabhaumaḥ | cittasya | dharmaḥ*; the visarga of *saḥ* is dropped before *ca*; *sārvabhaumaḥ + c- → sārvabhaumaś*.
- *sa*: *tad*, masc. nom. sg.; compatible with both *yogaḥ* and *samādhiḥ* of the preceding passage. The nearest antecedent is *samādhiḥ*; the connection with the sixth passage favours *samādhi*, but that argument relies on later text. Rendered “this”.
- *sārvabhaumaḥ*: masc. nom. sg., attribute agreeing with *dharmaḥ*. Formation by Pāṇ. 5.1.41 (*sarvabhūmipṛthivībhyām aṇañau*), in the sense of connection or portent; the senses “lord” and “known” by 5.1.42–43; vṛddhi of both members by 7.3.20 (verified by the author). MW *sarva-bhūmi* (L 238548–50): the whole earth; owning the whole earth; note *g. anuśatikādi*. MW *sārvabhauma* (L 243253–61); Apte (L 34884). The sense “relating to all conditions of the mind” is marked MW *Yogas., Sch.*, Apte *Yoga S.*, and is not independent evidence.
- In the next passage the same base *bhūmi* is the head member of *citta-bhūmayaḥ*; this may clarify *sārvabhauma* but does not necessarily resolve the ambiguity.
- *cittasya*: genitive; masc. or neut. by form, neut. by context. MW L 73536–46; Apte L 14935. English expresses the genitive overtly (“of *citta*”), unlike the Russian.
- *dharmaḥ*: predicate. MW L 99903 ff.; Apte L 17918.
- Article: the Sanskrit has none. The indefinite “a … *dharma* of *citta*” is used (author’s decision) because it makes no claim that this property is exclusive or unique; “the …” or “*citta*’s …” would imply uniqueness.
- Rejected: “all-encompassing *dharma*” (chooses a meaning); “a *dharma* of *citta* pertaining to all *bhūmi*” (decomposes the derivative).

</details>

> क्षिप्तं मूढं विक्षिप्तम् एकाग्रं निरुद्धम् इति चित्तभूमयः ।
>
> *kṣiptaṃ mūḍhaṃ vikṣiptam ekāgraṃ niruddham iti cittabhūmayaḥ*

*kṣipta*, *mūḍha*, *vikṣipta*, *ekāgra*, *niruddha* — these are the *cittabhūmi*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*kṣipta*: “thrown, scattered, distracted”; *mūḍha*: “stupefied, bewildered, at a loss”; *vikṣipta*: “thrown about, scattered, agitated”; *ekāgra*: “one-pointed”, fixed on one object, attentive; *niruddha*: “held back, stopped, restrained”. *bhūmi* in *cittabhūmi*: “ground; place, position; stage”; its meaning here is not established.

*kṣipta* and *vikṣipta* differ only by the prefix *vi-* (“apart, asunder”); the text does not say how they differ in substance.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *kṣiptam | mūḍham | vi-kṣiptam | eka-agram | ni-ruddham | iti | citta-bhūmayaḥ*. The original has neuter singular forms (nominative or accusative, not distinguished inside the quotation); the translation gives stems.
- Neuter gender: the labels may be thought of as attributes of *citta* (neut.), “[*cittam*] *kṣiptam*”; substantivization is also possible. An implied *cittam* is a hypothesis, not an established ellipsis.
- *iti* closes the list; there are no conjunctions. *cittabhūmayaḥ*: fem. nom. pl.; *bhūmi* is the head member; the relation is probably genitival.
- Dictionaries: *kṣipta* MW L 59526–30, Apte L 13369; *mūḍha* MW L 166125–35, Apte L 26487; *vikṣipta* MW L 194354–61, Apte L 29279; *ekāgra* MW L 39745–48, Apte L 10632.028; *niruddha* MW L 109412–16, Apte L 18957; *bhūmi* MW L 152108–21 (“step, degree, stage”, *Yogas.*), Apte L 25245. Senses marked *Sarvad.* and Apte *vikṣiptam* (which quotes this very sentence) are not independent.
- Collocations with words for mind outside the Yoga tradition: *manaḥ kṣipati* (Māl. 4.8); *…mūḍha-manasām* (Pt. 2.4); *pravikṣiptaṃ cetaḥ* (Māl. 9.8); *ekāgreṇa cetasā* (Bg. 18.72); none attested for *niruddha*.
- English: the list is asyndetic, like the Sanskrit (no “and”); the em dash before the summarizing clause follows English usage for an introductory series. The definite article in “the *cittabhūmi*” follows natural English usage (author’s decision) and is not to be taken as independent evidence that the list is exhaustive; the Sanskrit has no marker of exhaustiveness. The plural of IAST words is not marked.
- Not resolved in substance: the meaning of *bhūmi*, the difference *kṣipta* / *vikṣipta*, the relation *niruddha* / *nirodha*.

</details>

> तत्र विक्षिप्ते चेतसि विक्षेपोपसर्जनीभूतः समाधिर् न योगपक्षे वर्तते ।
>
> *tatra vikṣipte cetasi vikṣepopasarjanībhūtaḥ samādhir na yogapakṣe vartate*

Among them, with a *vikṣipta* *cetas*, *samādhi* that has become subordinate to *vikṣepa* does not belong to the category of *yoga*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*cetas*: “consciousness, thought, mind, heart, will”; a different word from *citta*, and identity of their meanings is not established. *vikṣepa*: “throwing about, scattering; inattention, confusion”; from the same verb *vi-√kṣip* as *vikṣipta*. “Has become subordinate” renders *upasarjanī-bhūta*: *samādhi* has become the subordinate member and *vikṣepa* the principal one.

The sentence speaks only of *vikṣipta*. It does not extend the exclusion from *yoga* to *kṣipta* and *mūḍha*. The restrictive “that has become …” does not assert that other kinds of *samādhi* exist.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *tatra | vi-kṣipte | cetasi | vikṣepa + upasarjanī + bhūtaḥ | samādhiḥ | na | yoga-pakṣe | vartate*; *vikṣepa + upasarjanī… → vikṣepopasarjanī…*; *samādhiḥ + na → samādhir na*.
- *tatra*: locative of *tad* (“there; in that; in that case”; MW L 81977; Apte L 16085); it refers to the whole preceding list, which *vikṣipte cetasi* narrows to one member. “With a *vikṣipta* *cetas*” renders the circumstantial locative, as Russian «при» does.
- *vikṣepopasarjanībhūtaḥ*: masc. nom. sg., agreeing with *samādhiḥ*. *upasarjanī-* is a *cvi* form: Pāṇ. 5.4.50 (short wording *kṛbhvastiyoge sampadyakartari cviḥ*; the condition *abhūtatadbhāve* belongs to the commentarial tradition), *-a → -ī* by 7.4.32; *gati* by 1.4.61; compounded with *bhūta* by 2.2.18 (verified by the author). *upasarjana*: “anything or any person subordinate to another”; in grammar, the subordinate member as opposed to *pradhāna* (MW L 36256–61; Apte L 10033). *vikṣepa*: MW L 194366–81; Apte L 29280.
- *yogapakṣe vartate*: *pakṣa* — side, party; set, class; position; alternative (MW L 113061–94; Apte L 19673); at the end of a compound *-pakṣe* also means “instead of, by way of”. *vartate* with the locative: to be, abide, be in a condition (MW L 204340; Apte); a weak alternative is “to be used in the sense of”. All readings give the same result: this *samādhi* does not count as *yoga*.
- Restrictive or non-restrictive. The Sanskrit attributive compound does not mark the difference; English has to choose. Adopted (author’s decision): A, restrictive, as in the text. The restrictive construction does not assert or prove that other kinds of *samādhi* exist with a *vikṣipta* *cetas*; no such claim is made, and none is to be inferred from the English syntax. Not adopted: B, non-restrictive (“*samādhi*, having become subordinate to *vikṣepa*, does not belong …”), which is also grammatically possible and may be read causally or generically; a prenominal calque (“*vikṣepa*-subordinated *samādhi*”), which leaves restrictiveness unmarked but shifts “has become” toward “has been made”.

</details>

> यस् त्व् एकाग्रे चेतसि सद्भूतम् अर्थं प्रद्योतयति क्षिणोति च क्लेशान् कर्मबन्धनानि श्लथयति निरोधम् अभिमुखं करोति स संप्रज्ञातो योग इत्य् आख्यायते ।
>
> *yas tv ekāgre cetasi sadbhūtam arthaṃ pradyotayati kṣiṇoti ca kleśān karmabandhanāni ślathayati nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti sa saṃprajñāto yoga ity ākhyāyate*

But that which, with an *ekāgra* *cetas*, makes clear a truly existing *artha* and depletes the *kleśa*, loosens the bonds of *karman*, *nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti*, is called *saṃprajñāta* *yoga*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*artha*: “thing, object” or “sense, matter”; *kleśa*: “pain, affliction”; *karman*: “action, deed” and its consequences; *saṃprajñāta*: “distinguished, accurately known”. “Depletes” (*kṣiṇoti*): the dictionary range extends to “destroys”.

***nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti*** (*nirodha*: “restraint, suppression, cessation”; *abhimukha*: “facing, turned towards; approaching”; *karoti*: “makes, does”). It can be read as “makes *nirodha* facing, near” or as “makes [something] turned towards *nirodha*”. English, like Russian, cannot keep this ambiguity, so the phrase stays in the original.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *yaḥ | tu | eka-agre | cetasi | sat-bhūtam | artham | pradyotayati | kṣiṇoti | ca | kleśān | karma-bandhanāni | ślathayati | nirodham | abhimukham | karoti | saḥ | saṃprajñātaḥ | yogaḥ | iti | ā-khyāyate*.
- *yaḥ … saḥ* (“which … that”): no antecedent within the sentence; by connection with the preceding passage *samādhi* is more likely, *yoga* is grammatically possible. Rendered “that which”.
- *tu* (“But”): contrasts with the preceding passage (*vikṣipta* / *ekāgra*); Apte sense 1 “but, on the contrary”.
- Four actions: *ca* links the first and second; the third and fourth follow asyndetically. The English keeps this grouping (“makes clear … and depletes …, loosens …, …”).
- *sat-bhūta*: *sat* — being, existing; real, true; good; the internal relation is not established. *pradyotayati*: causative of *pra-√dyut*, “illumine; make clear or manifest”; “makes clear” follows the Russian «проясняет».
- *kṣiṇoti*: √4 *kṣi*. MW (active): destroy, corrupt, ruin, make an end of; “diminish, waste away” is given for the passive *kṣīyate*. Apte II (active): destroy, afflict, ruin, corrupt; diminish, cause to waste away; kill. “Depletes” parallels the approved Russian «истощает»; the stronger senses (“destroys, ruins”) are recorded here and not chosen.
- *kleśa*: MW “pain, affliction, distress”; the fivefold list (*Yogas.*) is not imported into YS 1.1; Apte has no entry. *karman*: act, deed, rite; Apte sense 9, the consequence of deeds. *karma-bandhana*: relation not established. *ślathayati*: “loosen, relax”.
- *nirodham*: masc. acc. sg. of *nirodha*, or the indeclinable *ṇamul*. *abhimukham*: masc. acc. sg. or neut. nom./acc. sg.; *abhimukha* (MW L 12067–70; Apte L 3574): facing (with acc.); approaching, near; disposed to; favourable. *nirodha*: MW L 109427–36; Apte L 18958. Constructions: C1 “makes *nirodha* facing/near”; C2 “makes [something] turned towards *nirodha*”; C3 adverbial *abhimukham*; C4 *ṇamul*. The idiom *√kṛ* “turn the attention to” is recorded with the locative or dative, not the accusative. The *cvi* form (*abhimukhī karoti*) is not used in the text.
- *ākhyāyate*: “is called” (MW, Apte); *saṃprajñāta*: MW “distinguished, discerned, known accurately, *Yogas.*” (L 236301).

</details>

> स च वितर्कानुगतो विचारानुगत आनन्दानुगतो ऽस्मितानुगत इत्य् उपरिष्टान् निवेदयिष्यामः ।
>
> *sa ca vitarkānugato vicārānugata ānandānugato 'smitānugata ity upariṣṭān nivedayiṣyāmaḥ*

And this is accompanied by *vitarka*, accompanied by *vicāra*, accompanied by *ānanda*, accompanied by *asmitā*; this we shall set forth later.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*vitarka*: “reasoning, conjecture, doubt”; *vicāra*: “deliberation, investigation”; *ānanda*: “joy, bliss”; *asmitā*: literally “I-am-ness” (*asmi* “I am” + *-tā*), in the dictionaries “egoism”. “Accompanied” renders the participle *anugata* (“followed by, accompanied”).

The four qualifications stand separately, each with its own *anugata*. Whether “this” is accompanied by all four at once or there are four kinds, the text does not say here; the detailed account is deferred (“later”, *upariṣṭāt*).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *saḥ | ca | vitarka-anugataḥ | vicāra-anugataḥ | ānanda-anugataḥ | asmitā-anugataḥ | iti | upariṣṭāt | ni-vedayiṣyāmaḥ*. Sandhi: *-aḥ* before a voiced consonant → *-o*; before a vowel other than *a* → *-a*; *-aḥ + a- → -o '*; *upariṣṭāt + n- → upariṣṭān*.
- *sa*: nearest antecedent *saṃprajñātaḥ yogaḥ* (preceding passage); rendered “this”.
- Four predicates: masc. nom. sg.; *anugata* is the past passive participle of *anu-√gam*: MW (L 6189–95) followed by; following; tallying with; Apte: followed, attended; following; corresponding, in harmony with. The internal relation (accompanied by X / following X / corresponding to X) is not established.
- Tense: English “is accompanied by” uses a past participle and is closer to *anugataḥ* than the Russian present «сопровождается»; it still selects the passive reading. The active reading (“having followed, following”) is not excluded.
- *vitarka* (MW: “doubt, uncertainty” marked *Yogas.*); *vicāra*, *ānanda* (MW L 24352–53; in Vedānta “pure happiness”) carry no Yoga mark; *asmitā* (MW L 21522, *asmi-tā*, “egoism, *Yogas.*”; Apte quotes YS 1.17). The reading “four kinds” relies on YS 1.17, i.e. on later text.
- Simultaneous presence of the four characteristics is not proven; the text sets four separate predicates and does not say whether they are present together or are four kinds. The question is open.
- *iti* closes the statement as the content of *nivedayiṣyāmaḥ* (causative of *ni-√vid*, future, 1st pl.: “we shall tell, set forth”). *upariṣṭāt*: “later, further on (in a book)”.
- Rejected: “accompanied by *vitarka*, *vicāra*, *ānanda* and *asmitā*” (one predicate with “and” suggests joint presence).

</details>

> सर्ववृत्तिनिरोधे त्व् असंप्रज्ञातः समाधिः ।
>
> *sarvavṛttinirodhe tv asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ*

But with the *nirodha* of all *vṛtti* — *asaṃprajñāta* *samādhi*.

<details>
<summary>Notes</summary>

*vṛtti*: “activity, functioning; state, mode of being”; the dictionaries have no sense “fluctuation”, and the precise meaning within YS 1.1 is not established. *asaṃprajñāta* is the direct negation of *saṃprajñāta* (seventh passage). “All” qualifies *vṛtti*, not *nirodha*.

“But” contrasts this case with *saṃprajñāta* *yoga* of the seventh and eighth passages. Only *samādhi* is named here, not *yoga*. The text does not establish how this *nirodha* relates to *niruddha* (fifth passage) or to *nirodham* (seventh passage).

The English deliberately has no verb, like the Sanskrit and the Russian: neither “is” nor “there is” is supplied.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Grammatical analysis and sources</summary>

- Members: *sarva-vṛtti/vṛttin-nirodhe | tu | asaṃprajñātaḥ | samādhiḥ*; *nirodhe + tu → nirodhe tv*.
- *sarvavṛttinirodhe*: masc. loc. sg.; *[[sarva + vṛtti/vṛttin] + nirodha]*. The middle member is *vṛtti* or *vṛttin* (MW: *vṛttin* = *vṛtti*). Number is not expressed in the compound: “all *vṛtti*” as plural or as “the whole of *vṛtti*”. English “all *vṛtti*” (with no plural ending) keeps both readings, unlike Russian «всех». What *nirodha* means (restraint, suppression, cessation, etc.) is not established; “complete cessation” is not asserted.
- *asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ*: (a) “*samādhi* [is] *asaṃprajñāta*” or (b) “[there is] *asaṃprajñāta* *samādhi*”. Adopted (author’s decision): (c), the verbless construction with a dash, as in the text; it keeps both readings and the syntactic indeterminacy of the Sanskrit, and the parallel with the seventh passage. Not adopted: (a) “…, *samādhi* is *asaṃprajñāta*” and (b) “…, there is *asaṃprajñāta* *samādhi*”, each of which selects one reading.
- *vṛtti* (MW L 204501–19; Apte L 30713): mode of being; state, condition; being, occurring in; working, activity, function (Kap., Vedāntas.); mood of the mind (Vedāntas.); manner of thinking; etc. No sense “fluctuation”; no Yoga mark.
- *asaṃprajñāta*: MW (L 20735.1) “unconscious, *Yogas., Sch.*”; Apte (L 6072) “not well discerned, … a kind of samādhi”. Both senses come from the tradition; “unconscious” adds a psychological claim.
- Researcher’s observation (not an established fact): in the sixth and seventh passages the circumstance is a state of *cetas*; here it is the *nirodha* of all *vṛtti*.
- Rejected: “mental fluctuations”, “modifications of the mind”; “complete *nirodha*”.

</details>
